# Reference accounts

The owner named two accounts that did snippet posting well. Use them to calibrate what to excerpt and how tight the crops and captions should be. Never borrow their voice: drafts must sound like the owner (voice/profile.md).

Evidence so far is thin. X timelines can't be read from the cloud environment, so the notes below come from search-engine snippets of individual posts. If the owner pastes 15-20 favourite posts from either account, add them verbatim under "Examples" and update the notes.

## @BluegrassCap (Bluegrass Capital)

Bio: "Student of business models. Player of infinite games." Nashville / NYC, on X since 2014, roughly 45K followers and 50K posts per a third-party stats site (undated). Indexed posts include a compilation of Charlie Munger letters and meeting notes, a stock-evaluation checklist (management, culture, balance sheet, moat), and short conversational replies ("We read about culture all day as analysts but it's just as impt on the other side of the table"). Lens: business models, culture, management quality.

What to borrow: the selection instinct (passages about how a business actually works and how management thinks, not headline numbers), and the habit of resurfacing timeless letter passages.

## @WTCM3 (WTCM)

Bio says no affiliation, no advice. On X since 2019, roughly 14K followers and 9K posts. The pinned post lists research principles (read widely, wait for no-brainers, record decisions). Indexed posts are mostly a quoted passage in quotation marks plus a cashtag with little or no commentary, for example: "One fab requires as much water as the drinking needs of 17 million people... fabs and data centers in the next five years will require the power of the whole of India and the drinking needs equivalent of the United States" $ECL. Also data screenshots ("US sector positioning").

What to borrow: the eye for a striking scale fact buried in a document, and how short the post is.

What not to copy: the bare quote plus cashtag format. Under X's Original Content Rewards rules that pattern is the most likely to be treated as aggregation. Every draft here adds a take (SKILL.md rule 2).
