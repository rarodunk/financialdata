## 1. Quantitative thresholds he states or applies

He never states a formal numeric screen in this period. The thresholds below come from what he did in practice.

- **No fixed revenue-growth floor (change from the ≥40% baseline).** Names he held or bought were growing 25% to 262%. He kept Axon at 28–34% growth and called it "very steady". He judged Trade Desk at 25% too slow *for its multiple*, not too slow on an absolute bar: "Revenue growth rates of my top seven companies last quarter ranged from 25% to 206%. Guess who came in last, at 25%. It was Trade Desk!" [98922, 2023-11-30]; Axon: "Their revenue was up 34% in the Mar quarter after having dropped back to up 28% yoy… They are doing fine." [105695, 2024-06-01]
- **Lower return expectations, and therefore a lower growth bar, for the whole portfolio.** "I recognize that we will have to be happy (ecstatic even) to average results of 20% to 30% yearly again. That's probably the real world, and it compounds nicely." [108439, 2024-08-31]
- **SaaS-era growth rates are gone.** "the SaaS companies aren't growing at 70% per year any more because they have gotten too big for that." [106371, 2024-06-23]
- **Sequential growth becomes the main growth test. This is new and more specific than the baseline.** He annualizes the latest QoQ rate and compares it with the YoY rate:
  - Nvidia: "What's important to watch is sequential growth. If they can keep up the 17.6% for four quarters they will end up with about 91% yoy revenue growth" [105695, 2024-06-01]
  - Nvidia trim: "Well 15% sequentially compounds to 75% yoy… up 11% sequentially. The trouble is that 11% sequentially compounds to 50% yoy." [109876, 2024-11-01]
  - Implausibility check: "At 122% per year my calculation would be revenue in three years which would be 11 times what they have now… It just ain't going to happen." [109876, 2024-11-01]
  - "I'm just doing what I 'm comfortable with based on the sequential growth, and how impossible a continuation of 125% growth each year is." [109876, 2024-11-01]
- **Dollar sequential adds as a deceleration test (Monday).** A flat dollar increase each quarter means percentage growth must fall: "a sequential revenue increase of $12.4 million was enormous 11 quarters ago, when the previous quarter was $71 million (up 17.5% sequentially), but up $14 million is piddling this quarter when the previous quarter was $203 million (up 6.9%)." [105695, 2024-06-01]
- **Profitability and EPS are now required, or at least weigh heavily on sizing.** Unprofitable or high-PE names were trimmed: "I reduced the number of shares… because Samsara hadn't yet broken into making a profit, and Monday, although growing profitability very rapidly, still sported a very high PE." [98922, 2023-11-30]
- **Gross margin is cited but has no fixed floor.** He accepted Celsius at 48.8% ("up from 38.5%") and Nu at 43.2%, and praised ELF's 71% as "an incredible number for a company selling physical products." [97460, 2023-10-16; 105695, 2024-06-01]. The baseline's SaaS-level gross-margin expectation is relaxed for non-software companies; what he looks for is margin expansion.
- **PE and EV/S are used comparatively (see section 5):** Trade Desk "PE was 55… EV/S was at 17.2"; Celsius PE "24.5"; ELF "PE of 40, and an EV/S ratio of only 8"; Nvidia "PE based on the EPS run-rate of just 30" [98922, 2023-11-30].
- **Position-size caps (numeric):**
  - "I won't let any position size get over 25%, as a position size that large generally means that I have turned off my critical faculties" [96929, 2023-09-30]
  - ELF: "I had trimmed it recently when the position size got too much over 20%, but from now on I may let it grow a little." [106371, 2024-06-22]
  - Axon: "It was becoming too large a position, over 30%." [109876, 2024-11-01]
  - The cap effectively rose from 25% to about 30% of the invested pool once that pool had shrunk (section 6).
- **Major position = above 10%:** "I guess I consider anything over a 10% position a major position" [109078, 2024-10-01].
- **Guidance is discounted when the company sandbags.** "I warned against paying any attention to their comically low guidance… grew revenue by 71% yoy… and then forecast revenue growth for the year at 20% to 22%!!!" [105695, 2024-06-01]
- **NRR/DBNRR, Rule of 40 and PEG are never mentioned in this period.** NRR, a baseline core metric, disappears from his write-ups entirely.

## 5. Valuation: every statement about multiples

- **Relative PE and EV/S vs growth across holdings, used to trim and add (Nov 2023).** "its PE was 55, as high as Nvidia's. Its EV/S was at 17.2… So we can assume that it must have had one of the highest growth rates, right? … No, wrong." [98922, 2023-11-30]. In practice this is a PEG / EV-S-to-growth comparison across his own holdings. There is no absolute hurdle.
- **Celsius:** "its PE was 24.5, less than half the PE's of Axon, Trade Desk, and Nvidia, and a quarter of Monday's… a much more reasonable EV/S, at 11.0." [98922, 2023-11-30]
- **ELF:** "a revenue growth rate of 76%, a PE of 40, and an EV/S ratio of only 8. (Compare that with Trade Desk's at 17, with the slowest revenue growth rate of all)." [98922, 2023-11-30]
- **Nvidia run-rate PE method:** "a PE based on the EPS run-rate of just 30 (using four times the most recent quarter EPS)." [98922, 2023-11-30]
- **PE computation error and correction.** He first defended a trailing PE of 23.5 for Celsius, made up of 3 quarters of GAAP EPS ($1.81) plus an assumed 30¢ for the distributor-buyout quarter, "That would be $2.11 trailing… That gives them a trailing PE of 23.5." [98922, 2023-12-01]. Then: "So that $2.11 should go into $49.5 x 3 = 148.5 and give a PE of 70.4. MY ERROR !" [98922, 2023-12-01]. The correction is as he posted it, his own arithmetic. Relevant to the agent: he prefers adjusted or normalized EPS and will back out one-time GAAP charges.
- **High PE as a trim reason:** Monday "still sported a very high PE" [98922, 2023-11-30].
- **Price-based add (unusual for him):** SMCI at $498 "seemed like a hugely low price (after all it had hit $926…)" [108439, 2024-08-31].
- **GARP framing came through an outside source:** Sentinel bought on Hochfeld's "SentinelOne: GARP Valuation; Hyper Growth Performance" [108439, 2024-08-31].
- **Still rejects valuation-based selling when the price rises:** "Some of my good friends watch valuation carefully and sell and go into cash when the price goes up… I tend to buy more as the price goes up" [108439, 2024-08-31]; "for people who want to sell out every time their stock goes up and they worry it's overvalued… That sure beats trying to time the market." [103472, 2024-03-29]
- **No explicit numeric multiple hurdle is stated anywhere in the period.** Valuation is used as a *relative ranking tool between holdings* (where to put marginal dollars), never as an absolute buy/sell line. After Nov 2023 he rarely cites multiples again. In 2024 the growth-sustainability arithmetic (sequential annualization) stands in for valuation work.

### (a) Genuine changes in stock-selection method

These appear before the abdication and are applied to specific companies with explicit analytic reasons.

1. **Sequential-growth annualization is the primary growth test.** Annualize the latest QoQ rate, compare it with YoY, and trim when they diverge [105695, 2024-06-01; 109876, 2024-11-01]. This is the most precise new rule, and it is rooted in the Zoom lesson.
2. **Dollar-sequential-add test.** Flat absolute quarterly adds mean decaying growth, so exit even if management narrative is positive (Monday) [105695, 2024-06-01].
3. **Relative valuation vs growth across holdings.** PE and EV/S divided by growth are used to move money between positions (Trade Desk out; Celsius and ELF in) [98922, 2023-11-30].
4. **Profitability and EPS growth are weighted in sizing.** Unprofitable means a smaller position (Samsara), and very high PE means a smaller position (Monday) [98922, 2023-11-30]. "Replacing growth with profit" is acceptable (Monday 2024, Crowdstrike) [109078, 2024-09-29; 106371, 2024-06-22].
5. **Universe expanded beyond SaaS.** Any sector qualifies if growth, margin trend and EPS are strong (ELF, Celsius, Nvidia, Nu, SMCI, TMDX, Nextracker, Amazon). Nvidia: "decided to pay attention to results… instead of understanding its technology" [104667, 2024-05-04].
6. **Visibility and predictability requirement.** Exit when neither you nor the CEO can forecast order timing or size (Aehr) [97460, 2023-10-16].
7. **TAM-direction test.** Gaining share in a shrinking market is a sell (Trade Desk, ELF) [100013, 2023-12-30; 108439, 2024-08-31].
8. **Qualitative red-flag exits.** 10-K delay (SMCI), operational carelessness (Crowdstrike), IP theft or competitive infiltration (Samsara trim), capital misallocation (Axon HQ), partner dependence (Celsius/Pepsi) [108439, 2024-08-31; 101285, 2024-02-01; 110811, 2024-11-30; 106371, 2024-06-22].
9. **Speculation tier sizing.** Speculative names are sized at ½ to ⅓ of the core positions [100013, 2023-12-30]. Non-core cap of about 4% for businesses or regions he doesn't fully understand (Nu initially) [101285, 2024-02-01].
10. **Lower return expectations,** 20–30% a year [108439, 2024-08-31].

### (b) De-risking and attention reduction due to age or retirement

1. **Steady monthly transfers to the permanent out-of-market pool,** explicitly tied to "our advanced ages (I'm in my late 80's)" [104667, 2024-05-04]. These accelerated in Oct–Nov 2024 [109876, 2024-11-01; 110811, 2024-11-30]. By Dec 2024 stocks were about 10% of total assets [111857, 2025-01-02].
2. **Fewer positions and higher concentration (5–7 names, 25–36% weights),** justified by the smaller dollar pool, not by conviction: "I'm investing less. Therefore I worry less" [108439, 2024-08-31].
3. **Pruning tiny positions for attention cost.** Beam: "what am I wasting my time and attention for" [104667, 2024-05-04].
4. **Reliance on others' research** (Hochfeld, JabbockRiver, Bulwinkle, board write-ups) and skipping conference calls [108439, 2024-08-31; 108439, 2024-09-01; 109558, 2024-10-19].
5. **Re-using old ideas instead of researching new ones.** Samsara re-entry: "looked around at my old positions" [109876, 2024-11-01]; "I don't have the energy to investigate the many new stock ideas" [109558, 2024-10-19].
6. **Inactivity during illness.** Mar–Apr 2024: "partly because of medical issues, I again made almost no changes" [103472, 2024-03-29]. Sep 2023: an injury "interfered greatly with my concentration" [96929, 2023-09-30].
7. **Nov 2024 macro-driven selling.** This is ambiguous but best read as (b). The stated reason is macro uncertainty [110811, 2024-11-30], but it came one month after the abdication and alongside the stated plan to move to cash. It contradicts his long-standing anti-timing philosophy, which he restated as recently as Sep 2024 [109078, 2024-09-28]. An agent should **not** encode "sell on macro uncertainty" as part of his stock-selection method.
8. **Dropping long-term performance reporting and moving to possibly quarterly summaries** [109558, 2024-10-19; 111857, 2025-01-02].

The swings in this period are a mix of both causes. Nvidia was bought, sold and re-bought within six months; Samsara went from 22% to 0.6% to 26.8%; Monday went from 19.6% to 0.8%, back to 16.4%, then out. Each swing comes with a method-based reason, but the volatility itself fits the reduced attention he admits to.
