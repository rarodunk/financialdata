## 2. Metrics he computes or cites per company, and how he presents them

  - NET: "about 5.5 times".
  - DDOG: "about 4.9 times".
  - MNDY: "5.75 times" [89006, 2023-02-25].
  - Method: (peak price ÷ current price) × (current TTM revenue ÷ peak-era TTM revenue). Example: DDOG "$1,680 million is about 1.9 times trailing revenue of $881 million" and the price is "2.6 times" lower.

  - adjusted gross margin and its trend
  - adjusted operating income
  - adjusted net income
  - adjusted EPS (Enphase "$1.37… beating expectations by 15 cents and up 73%")
  - operating cash flow
  - FCF and FCF margin
  - adjusted EBITDA margin (Global-e "12%… last quarter was 15.7%")

## 3. Buy triggers and reasons for initiating or adding

- **Re-adds after earlier exits.** "I added back positions in Zscaler and Bill because of excellent and reassuring results" [92961, 2023-05-30]. Re-buying an exited name is explicitly fine: "It used to be hard, even for me, to go back to a stock that I had exited… almost like admitting that I had made a mistake. But who cares?" - **Adding into price strength (momentum confirmation).** "I tend to buy more as I see the price rise as a verification." [94904, 2023-07-29; 96050, 2023-09-02].

## 5. Valuation

- **Early 2023 stance: valuations were too high in 2021, are too low now, and are not used for decisions.**
  - "It's evident now that our companies were probably overvalued a year ago… as the Fool says, the best companies are always overvalued." [87419, 2023-01-28].
  - "our stocks are for the most part quite undervalued at present and the bots and sellers have way overshot" [87419, 2023-01-28; 89006, 2023-02-25].
- **Crude price-vs-revenue framing:** "on average, our companies are at roughly a third of the stock price that they were at in November of 2021, while their revenue, on average, is probably about 70% higher" [87419, 2023-01-28]. Feb version: "35% or 40%" of price, "60% or 70% higher" revenue [89006, 2023-02-25].
- **Per-company revenue-per-dollar-of-price vs. peak** (8.4x Bill, 5.5x NET, 4.9x DDOG, 5.75x MNDY) [89006, 2023-02-25]. This is effectively a P/S ratio compared with its own history. He used it to argue for holding (Bill: "That gives you an idea why, although I cut my position by two-thirds, I haven't sold out.") [89006, 2023-02-25].
- **May 2023 explicit change of method:** "For a long time, too long, in fact, I had been thinking in terms of growth of revenue almost entirely, with just a little attention to earnings and FCF. Now I'm giving much more consideration to Adjusted Net Profit, PE ratios, PEG, FCF, FCF Margin, EV/S, etc." [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Framed as a return to his pre-SaaS method:** "these latter metrics are the kind of metrics I had always used in the past to choose stocks" [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Admits EV/S got too high:** "Their valuations (EV/S) had gotten unreasonably high as everyone climbed on the bandwagon… I really should have listened more to Bear who continually warned about their high valuations" [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **The SaaS-cycle valuation thesis:** small IPOs "starting off at low or normal EV/S ratios because the markets hadn't yet recognized what they were. We rode them up" [92961, 2023-05-30]. He says new SaaS IPOs will not be cheap: "if they came out cheap everyone in the world would now be trying to buy shares" [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Valuation is used as a negative for Tesla:** "its stock is already very highly priced" [94904, 2023-07-29].
- **Aehr market cap is noted as small ("$1.5B"),** with no multiple computed [96050, 2023-09-02].
- **No explicit numeric multiple hurdle is stated anywhere in Jan–Aug 2023.** He never quotes a PE, PEG or EV/S for any specific company in this chunk. The valuation language is directional only.

- **Over-reliance on revenue growth:** "For a long time, too long, in fact, I had been thinking in terms of growth of revenue almost entirely, with just a little attention to earnings and FCF." [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Ignored valuation warnings:** "I really should have listened more to Bear who continually warned about their high valuations, but I didn't." [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Misclassified business models:** "We also had invested in some 'SaaS' companies that turned out to not be SaaS companies at all. I'm thinking of companies like Snowflake and Datadog." [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Wrong on Cloudflare's predictability:** "I thought they had a more predictable present than Snowflake but they proved me wrong" [91856, 2023-05-01].
- **Enphase hold cost him through opportunity cost:** "not exiting was a very expensive mistake, as its stock price kept dropping while the rest of my portfolio rose… Redeploying the money from a sinking company to a company that rose would have made a considerable difference." [94904, 2023-07-29]. *Lesson: after a guidance-driven drop, exit fully and redeploy rather than trim and hope.*
- **Stuck with the wrong names in Q1:** "I would have done a lot better if I had known about and taken a position in IOT or GLBE at the beginning of the year… instead of sticking with, SNOW, Datadog and Bill." [90807, 2023-04-01].
- **Bought the dip in Bill, then reversed it:** "I initially bought more Bill on the decline, feeling that it was greatly oversold, but after thinking about it, I sold back what I had bought and a bunch more." [89006, 2023-02-25].
- **Retracted over-strong wording on Datadog:** "I shouldn't have said 'I would have been negligent to not have exited', which makes it an absolute… I apologize for that." [90807, 2023-04-01].
- **Change in how he frames the 2022 crash.**
  - January framing (partial self-excuse): "You just can't say 'I should have expected this!' Some things happen that you can't anticipate or expect." [87419, 2023-01-28; 89006, 2023-02-25].
  - May framing names fundamental causes as well: "The little companies we had bought so cheap had become large companies and were hitting the Law of Large Numbers" [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Lesson on markets that are taking off:** "selling when the price goes up works in a weak market, but in a market that is taking off it doesn't work at all." [96050, 2023-09-02].
- **Lesson on oversized positions:** "a position size that large generally means that I have turned off my critical faculties, and it usually ends badly." [94904, 2023-07-29].

## 8. Macro statements and whether they changed actions

  - CEOs' "it will pass" line "helped me quit to focussing on the very short term" [87419, 2023-01-28; 89006, 2023-02-25].

  - Action: macro did contribute to concentrating the book ("I have gradually concentrated my number of positions in this macro environment") [87419, 2023-01-28].
- **March onward: macro changes what he selects.**
  - He prefers growth that holds up "even in these difficult macro times" and a "hard" ROI that is "very attractive in the current environment" [90807, 2023-04-01].
  - He downgrades services "that are easier for a customer to decide to do without in a tough environment" [90807, 2023-04-01].
  - *Macro became a selection factor through business-model resilience, not through market timing.*
- **Datadog:** "if I knew that this macro would be over tomorrow, or next month, own next quarter, I probably might have stayed in" [90807, 2023-04-01]. Uncertainty about how long the macro drag lasts was part of the exit.

- **Policy (California solar).** He warned a member in January: "California just took away some of the advantages that it had been giving houses with solar panels… You probably should research that before buying Enphase." [87419, 2023-01-28]. He bought Enphase in March anyway.
- **Ad cycle (Trade Desk):** "macro has really hurt advertising… growth should bounce when the macro does" [92961, 2023-05-30]. He bought into a macro-depressed name on relative share gains.
- **Macro as a cause of the de-rating (May):** "the Fed to come out with the largest, steepest interest rate raises ever… causing companies who are customers of our companies, to become very cautious" [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **E-commerce cap is justified by the economy:** "may be affected by economic conditions" [90807, 2023-04-01].
- **No market timing:** "Guessing tops and bottoms is not for me." [96050, 2023-09-02]. "sticking with great companies wins out in the end, and beats market timing" (every month).

## 10. Contradictions of the baseline rules

- **Revenue growth ≥40%:** he bought Trade Desk at "about 21%" growth on relative share gains [92961, 2023-05-30]. He also built Aehr to his largest position without citing any revenue-growth figure; Saul's own Aehr text cites only "net income positive" and a "functional monopoly" [96050, 2023-09-02].
- **Recurring revenue / SaaS only:** the book moved to roughly 50% non-SaaS by March [90807, 2023-04-01], and by August the top position is semiconductor test equipment. Non-SaaS names held in this period: Enphase (hardware), Global-e (e-commerce), Aehr (equipment), TransMedics (medical device), Tesla, Nvidia, Pure, Axon, Remitly. Of Aehr he says: "Yes, it is quite different than my usual companies" [92961, 2023-05-30].
- **Inconsistency on hardware:** "it's a hardware company" is a reason not to build Tesla [94904, 2023-07-29], yet Aehr, a hardware company, is 20.4% of the book [96050, 2023-09-02].
- **High gross margin:** he accepts 41.1% (Global-e), 45.7% (Enphase) and 66% (TMDX) [90807, 2023-04-01; 91856, 2023-05-01; 89006, 2023-02-25].
- **"Doesn't use P/S or EV/S":**
  - He now names "PE ratios, PEG, FCF, FCF Margin, EV/S" as considerations [92961, 2023-05-30].

- **DBNRR >110–130% as a core filter:** it is absent as a decision criterion. NRR appears only twice, as a data point (NET management target; Global-e 130%) [89006, 2023-02-25; 90807, 2023-04-01]. FCF margin, large-customer counts and "hard ROI" have replaced it.
